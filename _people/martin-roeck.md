For more than fifteen years I have worked where digital design, sustainable
construction and life cycle assessment meet. Digitalisation in construction
has mostly been used to build faster. I think it should also improve social
and ecological quality, because how we produce and use buildings sits at the
centre of the climate crisis.

<h2>How I work</h2>

Most of my work sits between science and policy. I develop the metrics,
methods and models that capture greenhouse gas emissions and other
environmental impacts across the whole life cycle, and I support the
legislative processes, much of it for the European Commission, that turn them
into reporting duties and target values.

Deep decarbonisation is not only a technical problem: it means changing a
sociotechnical system. So I try to keep three ways of scaling alive at once:

- **Scaling up:** changing the rules, by establishing the life cycle
  perspective in law and policy, as in the EPBD's whole life carbon
  disclosure and the EU Taxonomy.
- **Scaling wide:** building the data, networks and capacity that let many
  actors act at once, as INDICATE and the Global Building Data Initiative
  (GBDI) do, with particular attention to the Global South.
- **Scaling deep:** working with the pioneers of radical alternatives:
  bio-based and earth-based construction, low-tech design, community-led
  housing and sufficiency.

My rule of thumb for success: emissions from construction should at least
halve every decade.

<h2>Off work, off the ground</h2>

I live in Vienna with my wife and daughter, and spend much of my free time in

and flying paragliders on hike & fly and cross-country trips across the Alps.

<h2>Some lessons from the air</h2>

Flying is a good teacher. You cannot make the air do anything; you can only
read it, put yourself in the right place, and commit. Four lessons I take back
to work:

<dl class="lessons">

<dt>Read the landscape</dt>
<dd>Before intervening in a system, learn where effort turns into movement and
where it is absorbed. Good indicators, like birds circling in a thermal, make
the invisible visible.</dd>

<dt>Plan for where you will be</dt>
<dd>What is designed today sets emissions for decades, so the assessment has
to model the future, not the present.</dd>

<dt>Use the forces already there</dt>
<dd>A wing has no engine. Regulation, finance and market momentum achieve more
when you align with them than when you push against them.</dd>

<dt>Fly together, and stay patient</dt>
<dd>Other gliders show you where the lift is, as shared data and open methods
do in research. And even close to the ground, a small lift can take you back
to cloudbase.</dd>

</dl>

<h2>Roles and advice</h2>

I advise the European Commission, the International Energy Agency and the
United Nations Environment Programme on whole life carbon. I am Lead Advisor
on Embodied Carbon for the UNEP GlobalABC Global Status Report for Buildings
and Construction, and contribute to the forthcoming Assessment Report of the
Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC).

I serve on the boards of [Habitat2030](https://habitat2030.at/) and
[Wohnprojekt Wien](https://wohnprojekt.wien/), am a founding member of the
[Low Carbon Construction Association](https://www.lowcarbon.construction/),
and co-founded [wohnlabor](https://www.wohnlabor.at/), an association for
participatory housing research.

I hold a dual PhD in Architectural Engineering (KU Leuven) and Civil
Engineering Science (TU Graz), completed as a DOC Fellow of the Austrian
Academy of Sciences, and trained and worked as an architect and building
systems engineer.
