## Abstract

This research examines the adoption patterns and key factors influencing the
adoption of innovative sustainable building materials in housing construction
across socio-economic contexts in Cape Town, South Africa. In South Africa,
the adoption of sustainable building practices is hindered by socio-economic
constraints, technological limitations, economic barriers, and a lack of
awareness. Despite notable advancements in sustainable building practices,
several factors contribute to uneven adoption patterns, hindering the
widespread implementation of these practices. A mixed-methods approach was
used to collect data, employing semi-structured interviews and a survey
questionnaire randomly distributed to Cape Town residents and purposively
selected professionals working on housing construction projects. Furthermore,
the data collected were analysed using descriptive, thematic, and inferential
statistical techniques. The study found that most respondents from both
classes were aware of sustainable building technology and materials, and
perceived that environmentally friendly building materials help mitigate the
environmental impact of construction. They also indicate that, compared to
middle- to high-class respondents, most low-class respondents lack access to
resources such as government assistance and financial support, and both groups
often utilise recycled materials. The study found no significant difference in
the main variables influencing respondents' choices regarding sustainable
building materials across middle-high-class and lower-class groups. In
conclusion, this study advances the understanding of how socio-economic
circumstances influence the use of sustainable building materials and
technologies in housing construction at various levels. The study recommends
that governments and financial institutions establish initiatives through
training and financial incentives to equip stakeholders with the necessary
information, thereby improving the likelihood of adopting sustainable
practices.
