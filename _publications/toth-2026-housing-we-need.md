Europe faces four linked crises - climate and biodiversity, energy and
resources, housing affordability, and unhealthy homes - and this green paper
argues they have to be tackled as one problem rather than four. Its premise is
that the existing building stock is a resource rather than a constraint: about
85% of today's buildings are expected to still be standing in 2050, 20% of
dwellings are underoccupied while 16.8% are overcrowded, and the EU needs over
two million additional homes a year, about a third more than it currently
builds.

The paper sets out a hierarchy for action - renovate first, extend where
renovation cannot meet demand, build new only as a last resort - and
quantifies what following it could achieve. Using buildings better could
unlock 50–107 million dwellings and 4–9 billion m² of floor area, enough for
roughly 120–250 million people, avoiding 3.9–19.1 billion tonnes CO₂e over
fifty years and 5.8–13 Gt of material use. About three quarters of that
potential comes from renovation.

It is explicit about what it is: a theoretical and technical potential
assessment, which does not account for demographic trends, local market
dynamics or grid decarbonisation, and which acknowledges substantial
economic, social and regulatory barriers in the way.
