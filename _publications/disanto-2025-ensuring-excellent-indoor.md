## Abstract

The “Living Places” project involves integrating low-emission design
strategies with indoor environmental quality in single-family homes through
two key stages: design and use. During the design stage, the approach
established principles for enhancing indoor health and minimising negative
environmental impact by utilising advanced simulation workflows to evaluate
thermal comfort, indoor air quality, and daylighting. Life cycle assessment
(LCA) was used to determine the environmental impacts and drive
decision-making. In the use phase, an observation campaign was conducted from
1st July to 1st September 2024 with 98 participants. This campaign employed an
IoT sensor system to collect environmental data within living spaces and
included an anonymous survey to gather residents’ feedback on comfort,
satisfaction, and attachment to the houses’ spaces. The assessment combined
monitoring with survey information to confirm design assumptions and
understand occupants’ perceptions. Finally, the Living Places project aims to
provide effective guidelines for healthy and sustainable residential
buildings, demonstrating the feasibility of environmentally responsible
architecture.
