## Abstract

South Africa's rapid urbanization and economic struggles have led to informal
settlement growth. While informal housing provides shelter, construction
practices often do not comply with building and environmental regulations,
causing safety, health, and sustainability concerns. This study aims to
determine compliance levels of informal settlement construction practices with
these regulations. The research uses a qualitative approach, collecting data
on construction practices, materials, builders' skills, and regulatory
awareness through observations, interviewsin Khayelitsha, Gugulethu, Langa,
and Ocean View, while the focus group included informal builders from various
locations. Data is analyzed using thematic analysis to identify
compliance-related patterns, challenges, and opportunities. Key findings show
that residents and informal builders have limited understanding of and
adherence to regulations due to socioeconomic constraints, lack of formal
training, and the informal nature of settlements. Non-compliant practices
create environmental, health, and safety risks. The study highlights the need
for contextsensitive policies addressing non-compliance causes such as
poverty, lack of affordable housing, and low institutional capacity.
Recommendations include improving builders' access to quality materials and
training, supporting community-driven initiatives, and implementing flexible
approaches to informal settlement upgrading. The findings contribute to
knowledge on enhancing living conditions and promoting sustainable urban
development in South Africa's informal settlements.
