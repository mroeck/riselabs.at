RICS and a group of founding members have launched **CLEAR — the Coalition
for Life cycle Emissions Alignment and Reporting**, to align how whole life
carbon emissions from buildings and infrastructure are measured and reported.
The **Global Building Data Initiative**, which Martin Röck helped found and
serves as chief scientist, is among the founding members.

The founding group spans assessment, design, materials, software and
standards: 2050 Materials, AECOM, Arcadis, ASHRAE, Autodesk, Avison Young,
Buro Happold, GBDI, Heidelberg Materials, Mace Consult, Morgan Sindall, ORIS
Materials Intelligence, Turner & Townsend, WAP (an SLR company), WBCSD and
We2Sure.

<h2>What it actually sets out to do</h2>

<div class="findings" markdown="1">

- **Quantify the divergence first.** Analyse and measure the differences
  between the leading carbon measurement approaches, rather than asserting
  that they disagree and moving on.
- **Then harmonise.** Develop a framework for bringing assessment and
  reporting methodologies into line.
- **Then help others start aligned.** Produce guidance so that countries and
  policymakers building methodologies now can build them globally consistent
  from the outset, instead of harmonising painfully later.

</div>

It is positioned to support the Market Transformation Action Agenda and the
Buildings Breakthrough, which are pushing in the same direction.

<h2>Why we care about this one</h2>

The practical consequence for an assessor is a move away from modelling every
project from scratch towards assessments that are comparable, with models and
data that survive a change of jurisdiction. For the people building tools and
standards, it means building once and scaling across markets, with cleaner
interoperability with BIM and LCA software and benchmarking data that can
actually be compared.

This is the problem our own work keeps running into from the other side. A
[global buildings database](/projects/global-building-data-initiative/) is only
as useful as the comparability of what goes into it, and every benchmark study
we have worked on has begun by reconciling datasets that were never meant to
be reconciled. Harmonising the methods is the part that makes the data worth
collecting.
