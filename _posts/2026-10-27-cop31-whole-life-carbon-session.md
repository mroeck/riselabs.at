The **31st UN Climate Change Conference** runs from **9 to 20 November 2026**
in **Antalya, Türkiye**, under the Türkiye–Australia partnership modalities.
Among the built environment sessions is one on what happens after the
measuring stops: **"Turning Whole Life Carbon Data and Evidence into Climate
Action"**, which will present the work of
[CLEAR](/news/2026/clear-coalition-launch/), the Coalition for Life Cycle
Emissions Alignment and Reporting, of which the Global Building Data
Initiative is a founding member.

<h2>The argument</h2>

Whole life carbon assessment now produces genuinely valuable evidence about
how buildings and infrastructure perform. It has not, by itself, delivered a
single near-zero emission building.

That is the gap the session is about. Measurement is not management. Evidence
that sits in a report, in an incompatible format, or behind a consultant's
licence does not reach the decisions that actually determine what gets built:
a target, a regulation, an investment criterion, a procurement specification,
a market incentive. Making the evidence *accessible, connected and useful* is
a different problem from producing it, and it is the one that has been left
largely unsolved.

<h2>What the session covers</h2>

<div class="findings" markdown="1">

- **From evidence to instruments.** How whole life carbon evidence can inform
  targets, regulation, investment criteria, procurement and market incentives
  - and what has to be true of the evidence before it can.
- **Circularity in the same frame.** Supporting more circular approaches to
  the use, reuse and recovery of buildings and materials, rather than treating
  circularity as a separate agenda with its own metrics.
- **Connecting the scales.** How consistent methodologies, interoperable data
  and approaches such as Digital Building Passports link building-level
  evidence to building-stock baselines, to finance, and to national roadmaps.
  A number that cannot travel between those scales is not much use at any of
  them.
- **Alignment as the enabling condition.** How CLEAR and initiatives like it
  can support the alignment and collaboration that makes the rest possible -
  moving from carbon measurement to carbon management through proportionate,
  scalable approaches.

</div>

<h2>Where the emphasis sits</h2>

On rapidly urbanising and Global South markets, which is where most of the
floor area of the coming decades will be built and where the data
infrastructure for whole life carbon largely does not exist yet. "Proportionate
and scalable" is doing real work in that sentence: a method that presumes a
mature LCA consultancy market and a national materials database is not a
method those countries can adopt, however rigorous it is.

That is the same gap the
[Global Building Data Initiative](/projects/global-building-data-initiative/)
was set up to close, and the reason a benchmark derived mostly from European
and North American buildings is not a global benchmark.

<h2>Who it is for</h2>

Policymakers and government representatives; investors and financial
institutions; asset owners; standards organisations; built environment
practitioners; data and technology providers; and anyone working on national
building decarbonisation strategies - particularly in emerging and Global
South markets.
