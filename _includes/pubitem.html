{%- comment -%} One publication. Expects: include.pub M1. The title leads, because that is what a reader scans for; the author list follows in a smaller, muted size, with the team's names underlined; then the venue, year and DOI. Before this the entry opened with up to 32 author names, and on a phone the title was six lines down. The class names are `cite-`, not `pub-`. They were `pub-` and the text did not render at all on Martin's phone while the covers did - the signature of a content blocker, which is exactly what `pub` attracts: it is the French word for advertisement, and the widely used filter lists hide elements whose class contains it. The cover survived because `pub-thumb` happened not to match a rule and `pub-text` did. Renaming costs nothing and removes a whole class of failure we cannot reproduce or control. {%- endcomment -%} {%- assign p = include.pub -%} {%- comment -%} The full author list, every name, in the order the paper carries them. The underlining shows where RISE sits among them, first or twenty-ninth. Every form a person has published under, not just the current one. A name in an author string is the name that paper carries, and people's names change: Zuhaib published as "Zuhaib S" until 2022 and as "Batra Z" from 2024, under one ORCID. Matching only the current form would leave half of someone's own work looking like a stranger's. `cite` first, then anything in `cite_also`. The longest form has to go first within a person, or a shorter one can match inside a longer one and break the markup. {%- endcomment -%} {%- assign a = p.authors -%} {%- for person in site.people -%} {%- if person.cite -%} {%- capture wrapped %}{{ person.cite }}{% endcapture -%} {%- assign a = a | replace: person.cite, wrapped -%} {%- endif -%} {%- for also in person.cite_also -%} {%- capture wrapped_also %}{{ also }}{% endcapture -%} {%- assign a = a | replace: also, wrapped_also -%} {%- endfor -%} {%- endfor -%} {%- comment -%} `rise_work` says whether this is work RISE or GBDI did, or work the author did before joining. The filter chip that used it is gone (M1), but the field stays: the list still puts RISE work first inside a year, which costs nothing and is what a visitor scanning a year wants to see first. The thumbnail. Every publication has one: a title page where the licence allows it, otherwise a designed tile. Decorative, with empty alt text: the citation beside it says everything the picture does, and a screen reader announcing "title page of..." before every entry in a list of sixty-four would be noise. {%- endcomment -%}
