Expects: include.pub Any RISE person's `cite` name is underlined automatically. {%- endcomment -%} {%- assign p = include.pub -%} {%- comment -%} The full author list, every name, in the order the paper carries them. An earlier version shortened long lists to "lead author et al. (incl. ...)" to keep the line down. It read oddly - the brackets singled the team out rather than showing them in place - so the list is whole again and the underlining does that job instead: a reader scanning sees where RISE sits among the authors, first or twenty-ninth. {%- endcomment -%} {%- assign a = p.authors -%} {%- comment -%} Every form a person has published under, not just the current one. A name in an author string is the name that paper carries, and people's names change: Zuhaib published as "Zuhaib S" until 2022 and as "Batra Z" from 2024, under one ORCID. Matching only the current form would leave half of someone's own work looking like a stranger's. `cite` first, then anything in `cite_also`. The longest form has to go first within a person, or a shorter one can match inside a longer one and break the markup. {%- endcomment -%} {%- for person in site.people -%} {%- if person.cite -%} {%- capture wrapped %}{{ person.cite }}{% endcapture -%} {%- assign a = a | replace: person.cite, wrapped -%} {%- endif -%} {%- for also in person.cite_also -%} {%- capture wrapped_also %}{{ also }}{% endcapture -%} {%- assign a = a | replace: also, wrapped_also -%} {%- endfor -%} {%- endfor -%} {%- comment -%} `rise_work` says whether this is work RISE or GBDI did, or work the author did before joining. It is a field on the publication, written from each person's `rise_since` year and checked by docs-check.py, because the list has to sort on it and a template cannot sort on a value it works out while rendering. {%- endcomment -%} {%- assign rise_work = p.rise_work -%} {%- comment -%} The thumbnail. Every publication has one: a title page where the licence allows it, otherwise a designed tile. The three whose title pages are CC-BY-NC-ND have neither yet (H20), and they fall through to the tile drawn below in HTML, from the entry's own front matter - so a publication added tomorrow with no image still looks like the others rather than leaving a hole. Decorative, with empty alt text: the citation beside it says everything the picture does, and a screen reader announcing "title page of..." before every entry in a list of sixty-four would be noise. {%- endcomment -%}
