{%- comment -%} A list of publications, newest first. Expects: include.pubs include.sections="true" breaks them into kind sections. include.limit="6" keeps the first N after ordering, and turns on `featured: true` as the way of deciding what makes that cut. `limit` exists so a caller that wants a few cannot take them before the ordering below has run. The home page used to do exactly that (`sort: "year" | reverse | slice: 0, 3`), which sorts on year alone and so took whichever three the build happened to emit first inside the newest year. On 3 Nov 2026 that was a wider-team paper sitting above six of RISE's own, which is the thing the tier order exists to prevent. Pass the whole collection and a limit, and the cut happens last. {%- endcomment -%} {%- comment -%} Order: newest first, and within one year by tier - RISE's own work, then the wider team's, then what the authors published before joining. Someone scanning a year wants to see what RISE did that year before anything else. The tier is `rise_tier` on each publication: `core`, `extended` or `prior`. It is a field rather than something worked out here, because Liquid cannot sort on a value it computes while rendering; make-rise-flags.py writes it from each author's `group` and `rise_since`, and docs-check.py recomputes it every run so a stale tier cannot reach the site. This replaced a `rise_work` boolean on 31 October 2026. The boolean held `core` and `extended` in one bucket, so a year could open with a wider-team paper while RISE's own most recent work sat beneath it. Within one tier in one year the order is the build order. The files carry a year and no month, so there is no recency to sort on, and nothing here invents one. Liquid cannot sort on two keys, so the list is sorted by year and then rebuilt a year at a time, tier by tier. `pubs` starts as an empty array of the right kind, produced by a `where` that matches nothing: Jekyll's Liquid has no literal for one. {%- endcomment -%} {%- comment -%} `featured: true` decides what survives a cut, and only that. It applies when the caller passes a `limit`, which is the home page and nothing else today, and it is ignored on the full listing: a paper jumping its year on /publications/ would read as a sorting bug rather than a choice. It exists because the cut is otherwise arbitrary in a way nothing on the page admits. Seven of the 2026 papers are `core`, six fit, and which one falls off the end is decided by the build's order inside the year, since these files carry a year and no month. Checked against the live site on 3 Nov 2026: that order is the reverse of the alphabet, so alaux-2026 was last of the seven and never appeared. `featured` is how that is settled deliberately, exactly as it already is for projects and posts. {%- endcomment -%} {%- if include.limit -%} {%- assign lead = include.pubs | where: "featured", true -%} {%- assign tail = include.pubs | where_exp: "p", "p.featured != true" -%} {%- else -%} {%- assign lead = include.pubs | where: "__never__", "__never__" -%} {%- assign tail = include.pubs -%} {%- endif -%} {%- assign by_year = tail | sort: "year" | reverse -%} {%- assign years = by_year | map: "year" | uniq -%} {%- assign tiers = "core,extended,prior" | split: "," -%} {%- assign pubs = lead | sort: "year" | reverse -%} {%- for y in years -%} {%- assign in_year = by_year | where: "year", y -%} {%- for t in tiers -%} {%- assign band = in_year | where: "rise_tier", t -%} {%- assign pubs = pubs | concat: band -%} {%- endfor -%} {%- comment -%} An entry with no tier, or a tier not in the list above, would vanish from the page silently. docs-check.py fails on either, so this is a belt on top of that brace: an unplaced entry lands at the end of its year rather than nowhere at all. Three chained filters rather than one expression joined by `and`. The single `and` version broke the build on GitHub Pages: Jekyll 3.10 with Liquid 4.0.4 raises "Expected end_of_string but found id" on a compound `where_exp`, and because it is a parse error the whole site fails to build rather than the page rendering wrong. Chaining says the same thing and uses only what every other `where_exp` in this repo uses. {%- endcomment -%} {%- assign rest = in_year | where_exp: "x", "x.rise_tier != 'core'" | where_exp: "x", "x.rise_tier != 'extended'" | where_exp: "x", "x.rise_tier != 'prior'" -%} {%- assign pubs = pubs | concat: rest -%} {%- endfor -%} {%- comment -%} The cut, after the ordering and before anything is rendered. `plus: 0` because an include argument arrives as a string when it is quoted and as a number when it is not, and `slice` wants a number either way. {%- endcomment -%} {%- if include.limit -%} {%- assign lim = include.limit | plus: 0 -%} {%- if lim > 0 -%}{%- assign pubs = pubs | slice: 0, lim -%}{%- endif -%} {%- endif -%} {%- if include.sections == "true" -%} {%- comment -%} The kinds, in the order the sections and the tab bar both use. A kind with nothing in it renders no section and gets no tab, so this list can name a kind before any file carries it: `conference` is in that state today, and the tab appears by itself the moment a publication says `kind: conference`. Each section is wrapped so the tab bar has something to show and hide. The heading stays inside it, because hiding a grid and leaving its heading behind reads as an empty section rather than a filtered one. The id is the kind, so /publications/#data opens on that tab.
