{%- comment -%} A list of publications, newest first. Expects: include.pubs include.sections="true" breaks them into kind sections. {%- endcomment -%} {%- comment -%} Order: newest first, and within one year by tier - RISE's own work, then the wider team's, then what the authors published before joining. Someone scanning a year wants to see what RISE did that year before anything else. The tier is `rise_tier` on each publication: `core`, `extended` or `prior`. It is a field rather than something worked out here, because Liquid cannot sort on a value it computes while rendering; make-rise-flags.py writes it from each author's `group` and `rise_since`, and docs-check.py recomputes it every run so a stale tier cannot reach the site. This replaced a `rise_work` boolean on 31 October 2026. The boolean held `core` and `extended` in one bucket, so a year could open with a wider-team paper while RISE's own most recent work sat beneath it. Within one tier in one year the order is the build order. The files carry a year and no month, so there is no recency to sort on, and nothing here invents one. Liquid cannot sort on two keys, so the list is sorted by year and then rebuilt a year at a time, tier by tier. `pubs` starts as an empty array of the right kind, produced by a `where` that matches nothing: Jekyll's Liquid has no literal for one. {%- endcomment -%} {%- assign by_year = include.pubs | sort: "year" | reverse -%} {%- assign years = by_year | map: "year" | uniq -%} {%- assign tiers = "core,extended,prior" | split: "," -%} {%- assign pubs = include.pubs | where: "__never__", "__never__" -%} {%- for y in years -%} {%- assign in_year = by_year | where: "year", y -%} {%- for t in tiers -%} {%- assign band = in_year | where: "rise_tier", t -%} {%- assign pubs = pubs | concat: band -%} {%- endfor -%} {%- comment -%} An entry with no tier, or a tier not in the list above, would vanish from the page silently. docs-check.py fails on either, so this is a belt on top of that brace: an unplaced entry lands at the end of its year rather than nowhere at all. Three chained filters rather than one expression joined by `and`. The single `and` version broke the build on GitHub Pages: Jekyll 3.10 with Liquid 4.0.4 raises "Expected end_of_string but found id" on a compound `where_exp`, and because it is a parse error the whole site fails to build rather than the page rendering wrong. Chaining says the same thing and uses only what every other `where_exp` in this repo uses. {%- endcomment -%} {%- assign rest = in_year | where_exp: "x", "x.rise_tier != 'core'" | where_exp: "x", "x.rise_tier != 'extended'" | where_exp: "x", "x.rise_tier != 'prior'" -%} {%- assign pubs = pubs | concat: rest -%} {%- endfor -%} {%- if include.sections == "true" -%} {%- assign kinds = "article,book,report,data" | split: "," -%} {%- assign names = "Journal articles,Books and chapters,Reports,Data and tools" | split: "," -%} {%- for k in kinds -%} {%- assign group = pubs | where: "kind", k -%} {%- if group.size > 0 %}
