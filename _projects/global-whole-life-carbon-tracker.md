<h2>Context</h2>

The IEA, UNEP and the IPCC have spent two decades attributing somewhere
between 21% and 37% of global CO₂ emissions to building construction and
operation. Despite that scale, the IEA's Clean Energy Progress tracker still
classifies buildings as "more efforts needed", and building envelopes as "not
on track". As energy systems decarbonise and operational emissions fall, it is
the embodied emissions, from materials, construction, maintenance, renovation
and end of life, that increasingly decide the climate impact of a building.

Embodied carbon remains less well understood, less consistently reported and
less well governed than operational emissions. Global building emissions data
exists only at an aggregate level, or for subsets of the stock such as
residential buildings or fossil energy demand. Without a picture of where the
sector actually starts from, and of how far apart those starting points are
given different construction practices, material use and climates, there is no
basis for a common definition of what a "low carbon building" even is. Clear
data and clear definitions are the precursors to moving investment.

This project was commissioned as a follow-up to the
[10 Whole Life Cycle Recommendations for the Built Environment](https://globalabc.org/news/10-whole-life-cycle-recommendations-buildings-breakthrough),
published by the GlobalABC Materials Hub in 2024 and developed through a
consensus process with more than 100 experts from 40 countries. It supports
Recommendations 3, 4 and 6: increasing access to geographically representative
data and benchmarks, so that investment in reducing embodied emissions has
something to aim at.

<h2>Objectives</h2>

<div class="findings" markdown="1">

- **Build a framework that can be re-run.** A robust and updateable method for
  estimating and tracking embodied carbon from new building construction
  globally, situated against the wider evidence base on existing buildings and
  renovation. A tracker that cannot be repeated next year is not a tracker.
- **Resolve the world into 12 regions.** Differentiating embodied emissions
  across 12 world regions, which is what makes it possible to show where
  emission profiles genuinely differ and where the data is simply weaker.
- **Model around the data gaps rather than through them.** A hybrid approach
  combining bottom-up building archetype modelling with top-down material and
  economic datasets, because neither alone covers the globe.
- **State the uncertainty as a range, not a number.** Estimates for a 2024
  reference year, explored under a moderate and an upper-end material use
  scenario, so the uncertainty in material demand is visible rather than
  averaged away.
- **Test what policy would actually change.** The effect of material
  decarbonisation policies, set against the current policy trajectory and
  against a 1.5 °C aligned material emissions pathway.

</div>
