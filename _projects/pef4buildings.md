<h2>Context</h2>

The EU's **Product Environmental Footprint** method was designed for
products. A building is not a product: it is assembled on site from thousands
of components, lasts for decades, and its environmental performance depends
as much on how it is used as on what it is made of. Whether a product method
survives that jump is not obvious, and the answer matters, because benchmarks
and performance classes for buildings were being designed on the assumption
that it would.

PEF4Buildings tested it. The project was commissioned by the European
Commission and carried out by VITO, KU Leuven and TU Graz.

<h2>Insights</h2>

<div class="findings" markdown="1">

- **Two real office buildings, deliberately different.** A PEF study of the
  BelOrta office in Belgium, a business-as-usual new build, and of
  Baumschlager & Eberle's BE2226 in Lustenau, Austria, a nearly-zero-energy
  building. Different climates, very different energy performance - between
  them a test of whether the method holds across the range it would have to
  cover.
- **Then benchmarking.** An approach to benchmarking new office buildings and
  defining performance classes: how to define the reference building, where
  to put the system boundary, how many reference buildings are needed, and
  what to do with the range of options that remain.
- **Then the link between products and buildings.** How the assessment of a
  construction product's environmental performance connects to the assessment
  of the building it ends up in - starting from the two office studies and
  extended to other European building typologies.
- **Tested in the open.** Two full-day stakeholder workshops in Brussels, in
  July 2017 and January 2018, where the draft results were put in front of the
  people who would have to work with them.

</div>

<h2>Impact</h2>

The question PEF4Buildings asked is the one that keeps returning: a method
that works on a product does not automatically work on a building, and a
benchmark is only as meaningful as the reference case behind it. That is the
same problem the later
[European benchmark work](/projects/towards-embodied-carbon-benchmarks-for/)
and the harmonisation work through
[Annex 72](/projects/iea-ebc-annex-72/) were set up to address.
