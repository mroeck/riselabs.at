Most of the floor area of the coming decades will be built in China and
India. The data infrastructure for whole life carbon was not built with those
markets in mind, and the methods that work in Europe carry assumptions —
about material supply, construction practice, grid intensity and building
lifespan — that do not travel unexamined.

<h2>What we are doing</h2>

- **Building the relationships first.** Working with researchers, industry and
  policy actors in both countries rather than arriving with a finished method.
- **Testing what transfers.** Which parts of the global building data model
  hold, and which need rebuilding for local material supply chains and
  construction practice.
- **Feeding it back.** What is learned here reshapes the global data commons,
  rather than being an appendix to it.

<h2>Why it matters</h2>

A global benchmark derived mostly from European and North American buildings
is not a global benchmark. Closing that gap is the difference between data
that describes the world and data that describes the part of it that was easy
to reach.
