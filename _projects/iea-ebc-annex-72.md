For most of its history, building LCA has been a national practice. Scope,
system boundaries, reference study period, data sources and even what counts
as a building varied enough that two assessments of identical buildings in
different countries could differ by more than the thing being measured.

Annex 72 was the attempt to fix that, across 27 countries and seven years.

<h2>What it produced</h2>

- **Harmonised method.** Agreed assessment approaches, system boundaries and
  reporting conventions for the environmental impacts of buildings.
- **Workflows and tools.** Practical guidance and tooling, so the method could
  be used rather than only cited.
- **A shared evidence base.** Case studies assessed consistently enough to be
  compared across countries.

<h2>Our role</h2>

Subtask co-lead. Much of what the European policy work later relied on —
including the benchmarking and scenario work on this site — rests on the
groundwork Annex 72 laid.
