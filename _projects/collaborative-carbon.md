Collaborative housing — co-housing, housing cooperatives, community-led
development — is usually argued for on social grounds. This project asks a
narrower question: what does it do to carbon.

<h2>What we are doing</h2>

- **Measuring real projects.** Whole life carbon assessments of collaborative
  housing across Europe, using consistent method and scope so the results can
  be compared with conventional development rather than only with each other.
- **Separating the mechanisms.** Shared space, smaller private floor area,
  longer tenure, reuse and self-build each save differently. Lumping them
  together as "sufficiency" hides which ones actually carry the saving.
- **Testing what transfers.** Whether these savings depend on the tenure model
  or on design decisions any developer could make.

<h2>Why it matters</h2>

Sufficiency is the least developed of the decarbonisation levers and the
hardest to put in a policy instrument, because it is about building less
rather than building better. Collaborative housing is one of the few places
where it is already happening at a scale that can be measured.
