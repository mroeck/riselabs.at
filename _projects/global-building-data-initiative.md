<h2>Context</h2>

Building life cycle assessment has been done tens of thousands of times. Very
little of it is comparable, and almost none of it is public. Every benchmark
study starts by assembling its own dataset, which is why benchmarks disagree
and why most countries still have none.

<h2>Insights</h2>

- **Built an open data commons.** A structured, openly licensed database of
  resource use and upfront embodied carbon for new buildings, designed so
  entries from different countries and tools can sit side by side.
- **Agreed the minimum.** What has to be recorded for a building assessment to
  be comparable with another - scope, units, reference area, data quality.
- **Made contribution possible.** Routes for national teams, researchers and
  industry to add their own data rather than only consume someone else's.

<h2>Impact</h2>

GBDI outgrew the project that started it. It is now an association under
Austrian law with its own governance, and it continues as a standing
initiative rather than a funded piece of work.

In October 2026 it became a founding member of
[CLEAR](/news/2026/clear-coalition-launch/), the Coalition for Life cycle Emissions
Alignment and Reporting, convened by RICS to quantify where the leading carbon
assessment methods diverge and then harmonise them. That work addresses the
problem the database runs into from the other side: an open data commons is
only as useful as the comparability of what goes into it.

More at [gbdi.io](https://www.gbdi.io).
